[ ROBOTICS & MECHANICAL SYSTEMS ]

ENTRY 01 // 2026

Telescope Filter Wheel

A filter wheel for my telescope camera. It has nine slots: seven for filters, one empty for a clear view, and one closed slot that blocks the light completely. The design is modular, and an Arduino controls it. I also designed a companion app for the telescope. It pairs with the wheel, tells it which filter to rotate into place, and takes the pictures, so a set of shots through different filters runs without touching the setup.

WHAT WENT WRONG

Initially controlled wheel positioning using an open-loop step-count method. However, accumulated motor slip and step drift over multiple rotations led to filter slot misalignment. Resolved this by designing a custom mechanical indexing pattern that triggers limit switches upon exact slot alignment, providing reliable closed-loop position feedback to the Arduino for precise slot registration

ENTRY 02 // 2025 – 2026

Autonomous Model Car (In Development)

A custom 1/10-scale 4WD mechanical chassis engineered for outdoor autonomous navigation, powered by a Raspberry Pi 5 and BTS7960 high-current motor drivers. Features custom structural mounting brackets integrating an advanced sensor suite: a NEO-M8N GPS for real-world route navigation, an MPU9250 IMU for precise heading and orientation, and a COIN-D6 LiDAR dedicated to real-time environmental mapping and obstacle avoidance.

WHAT WENT WRONG

Ran into normal issues with component tolerances due to a lack of precision on some spec sheets, magnified through 3D printing. Also dealt with an issue on the 7.4V-to-5V step-down converter to make sure the microcontroller and 5V components could operate safely. Faced some problems printing the wheels with TPU as well, but it turned out to be a slicer setup issue rather than a design flaw.

ENTRY 03 // 2025

Humanoid Robot

Designed in Fusion 360 in one week and assembled in four days. Features 20 degrees of freedom (DOF) with a modular joint architecture actuated by MG996R servos, 3D printed entirely in carbon-fiber engineering filaments.

WHAT WENT WRONG

Had no issues during design and fabrication. However, while adapting the IR camera module to fit inside the head space, the light sensors broke; I desoldered them and soldered new ones to fix it. During testing, I noticed a lot of servo jitter in parts facing high stress because the hinges were mounted directly onto the servo shafts.

ENTRY 04 // 2023

NASA Perseverance Rover Model

Designed from scratch in under two months while teaching myself Fusion 360. Features a fully functional, 3D-printed mechanical rocker-bogie suspension system accurately mimicking NASA's rover geometry. Engineered with autonomous self-driving capabilities utilizing onboard sonic sensors for real-time obstacle avoidance, alongside live data transmission telemetry loops.

WHAT WENT WRONG

This was my first time 3D printing and designing, so I had a lot of trouble with tolerances. I also didn’t know about print layer orientation, so some parts that were exposed to high forces failed. All of these problems were solved through iteration, but most importantly, they taught me many skills that I now frequently use when working on other projects.

Astrophotography

OUTSIDE THE WORKSHOP // SINCE AGE 7

Pointing the telescope up.

I started astrophotography at age 7 when I received my first telescope, a 127EQ reflector. At 12, I upgraded to a refractor scope and began capturing planetary targets, alongside deep sky photography using a DSLR and a 55-250mm lens to pull in targets like the Andromeda Galaxy and the Orion Nebula. Now, I've got my hands on a vintage 1980s 8-inch C8 Cassegrain. With it, I'm planning to capture high-detail planetary images, build time-lapses, and explore concepts like planetary rotation using entirely my own collected data.

TARGETS

PROCESSING

Deep sky images are built from long-exposure image stacking (integration): many individual exposures, aligned and combined to pull faint detail out of the noise. Planets are captured as video and stacked from only the sharpest frames, a technique known as lucky imaging.
